Why documents

I have swapped the model underneath my Sunday system twice. Each time the system got better at the same job, because the job was written down in five files and the model was only the thing reading them.

Swap the files and you have a different system. Swap the model and you have the same system, slightly sharper. So this is the part worth your afternoon.

1

Set up the calendars and the label

20 minutes. Do this one on a computer.

The system can only read what exists. Before the interview you need three things: a family calendar the other adults can see, the school's calendar showing inside yours if the school lets it out, and a Gmail label that catches the school emails. If you already have them, this takes five minutes and only checks they are set up right.

Use a Google Family group. It is what I did, and it was far easier than building a shared calendar by hand: make the group and a calendar called Family appears for everyone in it. People who do not belong in the group, like a nanny, a grandparent or the other parent in a separate household, can be given the Family calendar on its own. The prompt asks about each person.

The school calendar is the unknown. Some schools publish a link you can subscribe to. Some keep it inside an app, or only in a PDF. The prompt helps you find out which you have, connects it if it can be connected, and tells you plainly if it cannot. Nothing here needs code, so if it cannot be subscribed to, your dates come from the school emails.

Paste this into an ordinary Claude or ChatGPT chat. It walks you through one click at a time, waits for you, and finishes by handing you a short block called SETUP SUMMARY. Keep it. The interview asks for it.

=== rules.md ===

## Order of work
1. School first: the school calendar, then the mail under the label.
2. Hold each school item against the family calendar.
3. Flag every clash: two things in the same hour, two sources disagreeing
   about a date, or a school item that needs an adult when none is free.

## Always
- Money, forms and bookings go at the top, under REMEMBER, with the amount and
  deadline quoted exactly as the source wrote them.
- Check for a receipt before asking for money. If a confirmation arrived within
  a few days of the request, show it as done instead of asking again.
- If a key fact is missing, say so. "No date published, check the form" beats
  both omitting it and guessing.
- Dress-up days and bring-something days get flagged the week before, and again
  in the week. Never the night before.
- Every weekday line answers the same four questions: who is out, who does
  drop-off, who does pickup, what is on afterwards.
- If nobody covers a handoff, say so plainly. Do not resolve it, do not assume.
- A line in household.md marked NOT SET is an instruction to say "not
  assigned" or "not confirmed". It is never permission to guess.

## Never
- Never invent a time, an amount or an address.
- Never repeat every email. Only items with a date, an amount, a deadline or an
  instruction survive.
- Never let an email's day-of-the-week mean this week. "Monday" means the Monday
  after it was sent.
- Never bury a fact inside a joke.
- Never cut a time, a place or an owner to make the message shorter. Cut the
  commentary instead.
- Never treat a quiet calendar as a quiet week until you have confirmed each
  named calendar was actually read.

## Radar
- One line. The single most important thing in the next two weeks.
- Worth it: camps, holidays, term boundaries, performances, anything needing
  money or a costume.

## What the page is for

The summary is what you read in the chat and pass on. The page is the one you
sit down with once, with a coffee, and it has a different job: a clash should
be visible before you have read a sentence.

Everything below serves that.

## Inherited, without exception

Everything in rules.md and voice.md applies here. The format changed; the
judgment did not. In particular:

- Never invent a time, date, amount or address. Quote it, or say it is not
  published.
- Check for a receipt before asking for money again.
- A cancelled entry means it is off. Never restore it from the usual routine.
- An empty mail sweep means there was no mail. It never means invent some.
- A date named in an email is relative to the day that email was sent.
- Facts stay plain. The personality goes between them, never on them.

## One thing it does differently

digest-template.md allows one line of radar, because a short summary has no
room to be wrong. The page gets a full four-week horizon table instead. A page
costs nothing to scroll.

### Clashes and decisions

Everything else on the page is reference. This block is the reason to build it.

- Walk the school sources first, then hold each item against the family
  calendar. Two things in the same hour is a clash. Two sources disagreeing
  about a date is a clash.
- One row per item: a checkbox, an owner's name, the decision in bold, then
  the detail that settles it.
- Name an owner always. "Everyone" is an owner. "Someone" is not.
- Order by what it costs to get wrong, not by date.
- Anything that changes the school day (a closure, a room move, medication,
  buses) goes at the top regardless of date. It affects the morning.

### Already handled

Bookings confirmed, payments receipted, appointments locked. One line each,
ticked. This is what stops the page reading as a list of demands.

If nothing qualifies, cut the block. Never pad it. If the mail sweep did not
run, say that instead. An unchecked list is not a clear one.

### The week and the weekend

- Monday to Friday as five columns. Each gets a strip naming who is away from
  home base, then a line answering who does the morning and who does pick-up,
  then the day's events.
- Name the person and the place, exactly as the calendar does. Never a bare
  "away". Never a bare "Home."
- The weekend names both parents too, day by day. Naming only the one who is
  travelling leaves the other unaccounted for.
- Every event keeps its time. An event without its time is an error, not a
  summary.

### The desk

One block per child: their name, year and school, then what the week actually
requires of them. Unread mail is worth naming. "Friday's newsletter is unread"
is a useful sentence.

Holidays, staff days and public holidays inside the next two weeks are phrased
as care impact: kids home, who is on.

### Weather: on or off, never invented

Two allowed states, and no third:

- sources.md names a forecast source: look it up, show the strip, and finish
  with one "what to bring" line that turns the numbers into actions.
- It does not: leave the whole block out. No strip, no "what to bring", no
  mention of the weather anywhere on the page.

Never estimate from the season, never reuse last week's, never soften a guess
with "probably". A forecast nobody looked up is an invented fact, and it is the
one invented fact people act on.

In the by-hand version the source is the app's own web search, named in
sources.md like anything else.

## The Verify tag

A Verify tag means: two sources disagree, or a fact you need is not in any
source. It goes next to the item, and the item states both readings and what
each would cost.

> Cast B is listed Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday. The family calendar only
> has Wednesday. If he is on Tuesday it sits on top of piano at 5:00pm; if
> Thursday, football starts at 4:00pm and arrival is 5:30pm. Verify

Two rules keep it meaningful:

- Never tag something the sources settle. A Verify tag on a known fact teaches
  you to ignore the tag.
- Roughly seven a page, maximum. Past that it stops meaning "look here".

How a page run works

Two passes, on purpose. Open the project and type:

run the Weekly Rundown

You get a plain list of everything it found: what it read, each day, every time, amount and deadline, and anything it refuses to guess. You can check it in thirty seconds. Tell it what is wrong, and when the list is right, type:

generate the page

You correct the list, never the finished page, because a finished page looks right whether it is or not. run the Sunday still gives you the summary in the chat, unchanged.

Keep the page private

It is a page about your children: their names, their school, where they are on which afternoon, and which nights the house has one adult in it. Do not publish it and do not share it by public link. The run is told not to offer.

You get one HTML file. Download it, then drag the file into your browser to read it, and print from there for the fridge. It needs nothing installed.

The red Verify tags
